## 1. Macro & Geopolitical Events

**The dominant story: the US-Iran War and its stagflationary shockwave.**

The United States and Israel launched strikes on Iranian military and nuclear assets, with the conflict beginning February 28, 2026. Iran's new Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei has vowed to maintain strikes on US regional bases and, critically, to blockade the Strait of Hormuz — the chokepoint through which approximately 20 million barrels of oil per day (roughly one-fifth of global consumption) normally flow. The market reaction has been severe and broad-based.

Equity markets are in retreat: the S&P 500 closed at 6,368.85, a seven-month low, down 6.8% in March alone — its worst monthly slide since December 2022. The Dow Jones fell 793 points to 45,166.64 and is in correction territory. The Nasdaq dropped to 20,948.36, also in correction. The VIX is surging.

Treasury yields have shot higher on inflation fears: the 10-year note finished March 27 at **4.44%** (highest since July 2025), the 2-year at **3.88%**, and the 30-year at **4.98%**. The CME FedWatch tool now shows market participants pricing **zero rate cuts** in 2026, with futures markets pushing the probability of a **rate hike** by year-end to 52%. The Federal Reserve's dot-plot already indicated only one 25-basis-point cut — markets are now pricing that out entirely.

The net macro picture is **stagflationary**: growth forecasts are being cut, inflation forecasts are being raised, and the Fed's hands are tied. Geoeconomic confrontation tops the near-term global risk rankings per the World Economic Forum, with 18% of survey respondents calling it the most likely trigger of a global crisis in 2026. The broader transactional US foreign policy posture is introducing a new layer of structural uncertainty into global trade relationships.

**Key data points:** S&P 500 at 6,368.85 | 10Y yield 4.44% | WTI crude crossing $100/bbl | 52% probability of rate hike by year-end
