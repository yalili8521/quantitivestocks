## 4. Macro Trends

**Federal Reserve:** Held the federal funds rate steady at **3.5–3.75%** for the second consecutive meeting in March. The dot plot still projects one 25bp cut in 2026 and another in 2027. However, revised PCE inflation projections jumped to 2.7% (from 2.4% in December), and the Iran conflict has brought energy prices directly into the policy discussion. The Fed is effectively frozen — cutting would risk stoking inflation, hiking would risk crashing an already fragile economy.

**Inflation:** February CPI at 2.4% YoY, core CPI at 2.5%. But these are backward-looking — the March/April oil price surge hasn't hit CPI yet. PCE and Core PCE are now projected at 2.7% for 2026. The Cleveland Fed's inflation nowcast likely reflects accelerating energy pass-through.

**Jobs:** Unemployment steady at ~4.4%. Job gains have been low but stable. The labor market is neither helping nor hurting the Fed's case.

**DXY:** The dollar is weakening even as yields rise — a **significant divergence**. The DXY has fallen ~5.2% since early April while the 10-year yield rose 27 basis points. This suggests foreign investors may be reducing US asset exposure, potentially reflecting concerns about the fiscal/geopolitical outlook. This is a key risk signal.

**Geopolitical update — Iran/Hormuz:** This is now the dominant macro variable:
- Oil supply loss: 12 million barrels/day (far exceeding previous oil crises)
- US gas prices passed $4/gallon; Brent crude surged 60%+ in March alone (biggest monthly gain since records began in 1980s)
- 400M barrel SPR release (record) + temporary sanctions relief on Russian/Iranian oil = emergency measures buying time
- Trump told allies to "get your own oil" from the Strait, offering no concrete reopening plan
- **Critical deadline: mid-April** — oil executives and analysts warn disruptions become structurally worse if Hormuz isn't reopened
- Analysts modeling $150–$200/bbl scenarios (Paul Krugman: "not crazy to go to $200")
- Iran drafting protocol with Oman to monitor Hormuz traffic — a potential de-escalation signal, but unconfirmed
