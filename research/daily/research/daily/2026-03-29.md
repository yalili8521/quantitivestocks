## 1. Macro & Geopolitical Events

### The 2026 Hormuz Crisis — Largest Oil Disruption Since the 1970s

The dominant macro force in global markets right now is the US-Iran war and the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz. The United States and Israel struck Iran in late February/early March, killing Iran's Supreme Leader. Iran retaliated against Gulf neighbors including the UAE and Saudi Arabia, and the strait — through which approximately 20 million barrels per day of oil ordinarily flows — has been reduced to a trickle, with only Iranian- and Chinese-flagged vessels able to transit. Commercial operators, major energy companies, and insurers have collectively withdrawn from the corridor.

**Key data points (search-confirmed):**
- Brent crude closed at **$112.57/barrel** on March 27 (WTI $99.64, briefly touching $100.04 intraday) — highest since July 2022
- Goldman Sachs estimates a **$14–18/barrel geopolitical risk premium** is currently embedded in prices
- Gulf states have collectively cut oil production by at least **10 million barrels/day**
- The IEA and market analysts warn the strait must reopen by **mid-April** or disruptions escalate significantly

**Equity market fallout:** The Dow dropped 793 points (–1.73%), S&P 500 lost 108 points (–1.67%), and Nasdaq fell 460 points (–2.15%) on March 27. The VIX surged to **31.05** (+13.16%), signaling genuine fear in markets. The 10-year Treasury yield held at **4.44%**, as bond markets juggle recession risk against an inflationary supply shock.

**Inflation trajectory:** The OECD now forecasts US all-items CPI at **4.2% for 2026**, up from a prior estimate of 2.8%. The Federal Reserve is caught between an oil-driven inflation spike and a slowing economy — a classic stagflation trap.

**Market implication:** This is a risk-off, stagflation environment. Energy and defense are the clear beneficiaries. Tech and consumer discretionary face headwinds. The mid-April Hormuz reopening deadline is the single most important catalyst to watch.
