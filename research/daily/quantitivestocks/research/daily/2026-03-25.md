## 1. Macro & Geopolitical Events

The dominant macro story today is the potential de-escalation of the U.S.-Iran conflict. President Trump announced that Washington and Tehran are "in negotiations right now," and a 15-point U.S. peace plan has been proposed. Pakistan is mediating, with an in-person meeting between the two sides potentially imminent. Markets rallied on the news: the S&P 500 gained ~0.5%, the Nasdaq 100 climbed 0.7%, and the Dow jumped 305 points. However, Iran reportedly responded negatively to the initial proposal, injecting volatility throughout the session.

Oil prices cratered on ceasefire hopes. Brent crude fell 6.1% to settle at $98.03/barrel, while WTI declined 5.5% to ~$87.50. This is a sharp pullback from the $112+ levels seen earlier this month when the Strait of Hormuz was effectively closed to commercial traffic.

Treasury yields fell as risk appetite returned. The 10-year yield dropped to 4.322% (from 4.37% the prior session), the 2-year settled at 3.883%, and the 30-year at 4.891%. The Fed remains in a "maintenance phase" holding rates at 3.50%–3.75%, but sticky inflation data and geopolitical risk have kept the curve volatile. Gold remains elevated at $4,560.13/oz, reflecting persistent uncertainty despite today's risk-on move.

**Net read:** Risk-on session driven by ceasefire hopes, but conviction is fragile — Iran hasn't agreed to terms, and any breakdown in talks could reverse today's moves sharply.
