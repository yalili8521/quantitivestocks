## 1. Macro & Geopolitical Events

### The Iran War & Strait of Hormuz Closure

The dominant macro event of the day — and arguably of 2026 — is the ongoing US-Israel war against Iran, which has led to the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz since March 2. Approximately **17.8 million barrels per day** of oil flows have been disrupted, representing roughly 20% of global daily oil supply. Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi declared on March 25 that "no negotiations have happened with the enemy until now," rejecting the US 15-point peace plan and extending the supply disruption into Q2.

President Trump pushed back the negotiation deadline by **10 more days**, but markets read the extension as a sign of stalemate. This triggered today's sharp equity selloff: the **S&P 500 fell -1.7%**, the **Nasdaq fell -2.4%** (now in correction territory, down >10% from its peak), and the **Dow Jones entered correction territory** on a near-800-point decline. Asian markets were mixed overnight (Nikkei -0.4%, Hang Seng +0.3%, Shanghai +0.6%), with Chinese industrial profits notably rising 15.2% in early 2026 on policy support — a pocket of strength in an otherwise risk-off day.

**Market implication:** Classic risk-off rotation — commodities surge, equities fall, yields spike. Structural engineering plays (pipelines, tankers, reconstruction) remain in focus until the conflict resolves.

### Fed Hawkish Pivot Risk — 10-Year Yield at ~4.41%

The Federal Reserve held rates unchanged and guided for just **one 25 bps cut in 2026**, but the oil-driven inflation shock is now shifting the calculus dramatically. As of today, **markets are pricing approximately a 50% chance of a Fed rate hike by December** — a sharp reversal from the two-cut scenario priced just months ago. The **10-year Treasury yield sits at ~4.41%**, near 8-month highs. The **2-year yield slid to ~3.91%** as a flight to safety bid emerged intraday. Gold is trading at **$4,433/oz**, off its January all-time high of ~$5,589 but well-supported by geopolitical risk. Rising yields and a stronger dollar are competing with the war-risk bid, keeping gold in a volatile holding pattern.

**Macro regime: Risk-off.** Inflation fears + potential Fed hike + war uncertainty = hawkish risk-off. Equities, crypto, and long-duration bonds are being hit simultaneously.
