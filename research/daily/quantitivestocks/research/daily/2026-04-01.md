Policymakers still project one cut in 2026, but market pricing from March 30 showed 52% probability of a rate hike by year-end. The disconnect between Fed guidance and market pricing reflects genuine uncertainty about the stagflationary impulse from the Iran war.

**Labor Market:** The Fed noted job gains have remained low and unemployment is little changed — consistent with a cooling but not collapsing labor market. The stagflation signal is growth slowing while inflation risks rise.

**Dollar Strength (DXY):** The US dollar index broke past 100 for the first time since May 2025, gaining nearly 3% in March alone. The driver is safe-haven demand amid the Iran conflict, not growth optimism — an important distinction. Dollar strength is a headwind for commodities, EM, and crypto priced in USD.

**Geopolitical:** The Iran war and Strait of Hormuz blockade remain the dominant macro catalyst. No signs of de-escalation. Oil above $100/bbl. The FOMC explicitly flagged Middle East developments as creating economic uncertainty.
