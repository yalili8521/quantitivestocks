## 1. Macro & Geopolitical Events

The dominant macro theme as of March 28, 2026, is stagflation risk driven by the ongoing U.S.–Israel–Iran war, which escalated sharply in late February and has fundamentally repriced the risk environment. The Strait of Hormuz — through which an estimated **17.8 million barrels per day** of oil flows — has been effectively closed to commercial traffic since March 2, with Iran now operating a "toll booth" system for select Chinese and Russian vessels. This is a direct structural supply shock, not a temporary headline.

**Equity market carnage is accelerating.** On Friday, March 27, the Dow Jones fell **793.47 points (–1.73%)** to 45,166.64, the S&P 500 dropped **–1.67%** to 6,368.85, and the Nasdaq slid **–2.15%** to 20,948.36 — the fifth straight weekly decline for the broad market, down 2.1% for the week. Dow futures this morning (March 28) are indicating a further **–596 points (–1.29%)** open, suggesting no relief yet. The VIX is surging.

**Rates are flashing hawkish.** The 10-year Treasury yield is hovering near **4.42%** — an 8-month high — while the 2-year yield has spiked nearly 60 basis points to **3.96%**, with the yield curve uninverting in rate-hike fashion. Critically, futures market probability of a **Fed rate hike by end of 2026** crossed 50% for the first time this cycle (CME FedWatch), even as the economy shows signs of softening. Consumer sentiment fell to **53.3** in late March, down 5.8% from February. This is the stagflation setup: an inflation-driven energy shock hitting simultaneously with deteriorating growth expectations.

**Investment implications:** Risk assets broadly face headwinds. The stagflation playbook favors energy, commodities (especially gold), defense, and inflation-resistant real assets — while punishing long-duration tech, growth, and bonds. This is a rotation environment, not a sell-everything environment for the right sectors.

## 3. Energy Sector

This is the clearest sector beneficiary of the current environment, and the data confirms it decisively. As of March 28, 2026:

- **Brent crude: $112.57/barrel (+4.22%)**
- **WTI: $99.64/barrel (+5.46%)**, briefly touching $100.04 intraday
- Goldman Sachs estimates a **$14–18/barrel geopolitical risk premium** baked into current prices

The oil market is in **backwardation** — front-month contracts trading above forward contracts — signaling tight immediate supply with traders expecting eventual normalization. However, "eventual" is doing a lot of work there: the Strait of Hormuz disruption affecting 17.8M bbl/day has no clear resolution timeline. Iran has explicitly stated "no negotiations have happened," and the toll-booth system targeting only Chinese and Russian vessels is a deliberate economic warfare tool.

The **natural gas pipeline buildout** adds a structural angle: the U.S. is set to add approximately **18 Bcf/day of new pipeline capacity in 2026**, the most since 2008. Kinder Morgan is deploying **$3.4 billion in 2026 capex** on pipeline expansion, with the Gulf Coast Express expansion, Blackcomb Pipeline (2.5 Bcf/day), and Hugh Brinson Pipeline (1.5 Bcf/day first phase) all coming online in H2 2026. Permian Basin Waha Hub prices have been negative for 25 straight days due to existing constraints — relief is coming but not yet here.

Energy stocks are the leading sector performer of 2026. **XLE** (Energy Select Sector SPDR ETF) was trading at **~$62.62** on March 27, up from ~$44.20 at the start of the year — a ~42% YTD gain. The dividend yield is 2.5% and P/E is 21.81. Most of the YTD gains preceded the Iran attack, but the Hormuz closure has added a new leg.

**Broader Play (ETFs only):**

- **VDE** (Vanguard Energy ETF): Broader energy basket including midstream and E&P; confirmed as a leading sector ETF in 2026.
